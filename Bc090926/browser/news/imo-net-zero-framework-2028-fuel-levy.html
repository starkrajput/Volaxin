IMO Net-Zero Framework Sets 2028 Start for Marine Fuel Levy

Owners face a per-tonne charge on well-to-wake emissions from 2028, with the first compliance year landing mid-cycle for most drydock plans.

Published 5 Sept 2026 7 min read By Aditya London

The IMO has agreed a two-tier pricing mechanism on marine fuel emissions starting 2028. Here is what it costs, who pays, and which vessels are most exposed.

Key takeaways
  • The framework starts in 2028 and applies to vessels above 5,000 GT, matching the existing DCS reporting boundary.
  • It prices well-to-wake GHG intensity, not fuel volume, so dual-fuel tonnage is treated differently.
  • Ships beating the declining trajectory bank tradable surplus units; shortfalls buy remedial units.
  • Older steam-turbine LNG carriers and mid-life bulkers carry the highest per-voyage exposure.
In short

The IMO net-zero framework begins charging ships over 5,000 GT for well-to-wake greenhouse gas intensity from 2028. Vessels beating the declining trajectory bank tradable surplus units; those falling short buy remedial units at a fixed tier price settled annually.

Shipowners now have a firm date.

What the framework actually charges

The mechanism prices well-to-wake greenhouse gas intensity rather than fuel volume, which is a meaningful distinction for operators running LNG dual-fuel tonnage. Vessels above 5,000 GT fall in scope, matching the existing DCS reporting boundary, so the administrative footprint is already largely in place for most fleets. The charge applies against a declining GHG intensity trajectory, and ships beating the trajectory generate surplus units that can be banked or traded. Ships falling short buy remedial units at a fixed tier price. Because the baseline tightens annually, a vessel that is compliant in 2028 is not automatically compliant in 2031 without efficiency work or a fuel switch.

The levy is not a fuel tax. It is an intensity mechanism with a cash settlement.

Which vessels are most exposed

Older steam-turbine LNG carriers and mid-life bulkers on conventional fuel oil carry the highest per-voyage exposure, because their baseline intensity sits furthest above the declining trajectory. Container tonnage is comparatively better placed where operators have already invested in engine derating and slow-steaming schedules. The exposure calculation is not simply age: a well-maintained 15-year-old vessel with optimised hull coatings and a modern propeller can outperform a poorly maintained 8-year-old ship. Operators should model exposure per trading pattern, since ballast-heavy rotations amplify intensity penalties.

What to watch next

Three signals matter over the next eighteen months.

  • MEPC guidance on surplus-unit trading mechanics, expected 2027
  • National implementation rules for flag administrations
  • Bunker supplier readiness for certified low-intensity blends

Frequently asked

When does the IMO net-zero framework start?

The mechanism begins in 2028, with the first full compliance year running on the calendar year and settling annually thereafter against a declining GHG intensity trajectory.

Which vessels are in scope?

Vessels above 5,000 gross tonnage, which aligns with the existing IMO Data Collection System reporting boundary, so most reporting infrastructure already exists.
