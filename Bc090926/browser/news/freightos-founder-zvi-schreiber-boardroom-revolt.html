Freightos Founder Zvi Schreiber Launches Boardroom Revolt Over Growth Strategy

Former board member and digital booking platform founder pushes for sweeping leadership overhaul amid collapsing shareholder value.

Published 9 Sept 2026 3 min read By Aditya

Freightos founder Zvi Schreiber has initiated a public campaign to restructure the digital freight platform's board, citing stalled growth and collapsing shareholder value.

Key takeaways
  • Freightos founder Zvi Schreiber launched a public campaign in September 2026 to overhaul the digital freight platform's board.
  • Schreiber previously resigned from his board seat at the box booking platform in February.
  • The dispute was triggered by management's decision to designate 2026 as a transition year rather than pursuing aggressive growth.
  • Schreiber argues that current leadership has allowed shareholder value to collapse through flawed strategic decisions.
In short

Freightos founder Zvi Schreiber launched a public boardroom revolt to overhaul the digital freight platform's board, citing abandoned growth, poor strategic direction, and a collapse in shareholder value following management's decision to treat 2026 as a transition year.

Freightos founder Zvi Schreiber has launched a high-stakes public campaign to overhaul the digital freight platform's board of directors, according to Splash247. Schreiber, who previously resigned from his board seat in February, argues that current leadership has abandoned aggressive expansion strategies, backed the wrong commercial direction, and overseen a severe collapse in shareholder value for the box booking platform. The dispute centers heavily on corporate vision, with Schreiber publicly challenging management's controversial decision to designate 2026 as a sluggish "transition year" rather than pressing forward with commercial scaling.

Why is the Freightos founder staging a boardroom revolt?

Freightos founder Zvi Schreiber is staging a public boardroom revolt because he believes current management has steered the digital booking platform away from necessary growth and allowed shareholder value to plummet. Having resigned from the board in February, Schreiber took his grievances public after leadership labeled 2026 a "transition year" for the company. This designation signaled to the founder that the company was sidelining rapid scaling in favor of defensive positioning, prompting an urgent demand for board-level restructuring and a return to aggressive commercial expansion within the container shipping sector.

For fleet managers, digital charterers, and maritime tech investors, the public feud highlights the ongoing execution risks facing digital logistics firms trying to monetize containerized freight booking networks. As digital freight platforms mature, the tension between long-term technological investment and immediate financial performance often exposes deep rifts between founding visionaries and institutional directors.

How has collapsing shareholder value fueled the crisis?

Collapsing shareholder value has served as the primary catalyst for the Freightos boardroom rebellion, as market disillusionment mounts over the digital booking platform's strategic trajectory. Zvi Schreiber's public intervention underscores widespread investor frustration regarding how leadership manages operational milestones and revenue growth in an increasingly competitive freight rate environment. When founders of maritime tech platforms return to active public opposition, it typically signals a breakdown in private boardroom consensus regarding capital allocation and executive accountability.

What to watch next

Industry observers and maritime tech investors tracking the Freightos boardroom dispute should monitor three concrete milestones over the coming months. First, watch for any upcoming extraordinary general meetings or shareholder proxy filings called by Zvi Schreiber or institutional investors. Second, track upcoming quarterly financial earnings reports to see if management defends the 2026 transition strategy or adjusts guidance. Finally, pay attention to potential changes within the board composition or executive suite as institutional shareholders weigh stability against strategic pivots.
