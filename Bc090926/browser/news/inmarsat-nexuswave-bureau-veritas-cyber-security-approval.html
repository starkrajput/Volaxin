Inmarsat NexusWave Secures Bureau Veritas Cyber Security Approval

Class society validation under IACS UR E27 gives shipowners a smoother path to deploying multi-orbit connectivity across mixed fleets.

Published 9 Sept 2026 3 min read By Marcus

Inmarsat Maritime secures Bureau Veritas cyber security approval for its NexusWave connectivity service, meeting strict IACS UR E27 standards.

Key takeaways
  • Inmarsat Maritime secured Cyber Security Type Approval from Bureau Veritas for its NexusWave connectivity service.
  • The approval verifies compliance with IACS Unified Requirement E27 (UR E27) for onboard cyber resilience.
  • The evaluation covered onboard ICT architecture, network routing equipment, and managed service edge infrastructure.
  • NexusWave previously secured a similar type approval from Japanese classification society ClassNK earlier this year.
  • The service bonds GEO, LEO, LTE, and L-band networks into a single managed connectivity platform for vessels.
In short

Inmarsat Maritime received Cyber Security Type Approval from Bureau Veritas for its NexusWave connectivity service. The validation confirms compliance with IACS Unified Requirement E27 (UR E27), covering onboard ICT architecture, network equipment, and managed service edge infrastructure.

Shipowners rolling out multi-orbit connectivity across diverse fleets now have a clearer path to compliance following fresh class validation for Inmarsat's managed network platform. Inmarsat Maritime has secured official Cyber Security Type Approval from Bureau Veritas for its NexusWave connectivity service, verifying that the system aligns with rigorous international standards for onboard digital resilience. The independent assessment provides technical assurance that complex satellite and terrestrial communications networks meet baseline security benchmarks required by major class societies, easing the regulatory burden for maritime operators.

According to Container News, the Bureau Veritas evaluation centered on the IACS Unified Requirement E27, commonly known as IACS UR E27, which governs cyber resilience for onboard systems and equipment. The classification society reviewed the complete security architecture and control mechanisms underpinning NexusWave, examining onboard ICT hardware, network routing equipment, and central orchestration processes. Additionally, auditors scrutinized the managed service edge infrastructure to verify how disparate components integrate into a cohesive, secure operating environment. This comprehensive review ensures that vessel networks maintain robust defense mechanisms against escalating digital threats without compromising operational efficiency.

How NexusWave combines multiple networks safely

NexusWave functions as an advanced managed maritime connectivity service designed to aggregate several distinct network layers into a single, high-performance pipe for commercial shipping operations. The platform bonds Geostationary Earth Orbit and Low Earth Orbit satellite links alongside terrestrial LTE and legacy L-band connectivity, ensuring continuous uptime for essential vessel operations, corporate management systems, and crew welfare networks. Because these distinct communication channels feed into a unified onboard architecture, maintaining strict cybersecurity protocols is vital to prevent unauthorized access or lateral movement across sensitive shipboard domains. To mitigate these operational risks, the platform incorporates advanced native security features such as stringent network segmentation, robust encryption protocols, and centrally managed administrative controls that allow fleet superintendents to monitor threat vectors in real time across global trading routes.

"Cyber resilience is becoming an increasingly important consideration for connected vessels navigating strict international compliance frameworks."

What does BV approval mean for shipowners?

The Bureau Veritas type approval directly simplifies the administrative and technical hurdles shipowners face when specifying digital upgrades for newbuildings or retrofitting existing tonnage under varied flag states. Fleet managers often encounter friction when deploying advanced IT infrastructure because individual class societies enforce unique interpretation rules regarding onboard network security. By earning both this fresh Bureau Veritas validation and an earlier ClassNK approval secured earlier this year, Inmarsat has effectively eliminated a major compliance bottleneck for international operators. Shipowners can now deploy the managed connectivity service across mixed fleets without triggering costly, time-consuming individual vessel audits or custom engineering reviews for network integration.

  • Validates compliance with IACS UR E27 cyber resilience benchmarks.
  • Covers onboard ICT architecture, edge infrastructure, and network orchestration.
  • Follows a prior classification approval secured earlier this year from ClassNK.
  • Simplifies fleet-wide digital rollouts across vessels with diverse flag and class requirements.

What to watch next

As maritime digitalization accelerates under tightening regulatory scrutiny, maritime compliance officers and fleet superintendents should monitor three key milestones regarding onboard cybersecurity standards and multi-orbit network deployments. First, track how additional IACS member societies adopt and interpret UR E27 compliance for retrofitted tonnage during upcoming annual class surveys. Second, observe whether competing managed service providers accelerate their own class certification timelines to match Inmarsat's dual Bureau Veritas and ClassNK approvals. Third, watch for potential updates from flag administrations regarding mandatory enforcement dates for onboard network segmentation and cyber risk management under updated ISM Code guidelines.
