Hapag-Lloyd Warns Shippers Over India SCMTR Compliance Deadlines

Navigating the full rollout of India's Sea Cargo Manifest and Transshipment Regulations requires absolute data consistency across all shipping bills.

Published 9 Sept 2026 3 min read By Marcus Mumbai

Hapag-Lloyd issues urgent compliance warnings as all Indian ports transition to the mandatory SCMTR framework, impacting manifests and cargo documentation.

Key takeaways
  • All Indian ports are now operating under the mandatory SCMTR framework according to Hapag-Lloyd.
  • Import information for the Sea Arrival Manifest must be submitted 96 hours before vessel arrival.
  • Shippers are responsible for filing the Export General Manifest directly if data is submitted late or incorrectly.
  • Mandatory information required includes HSN codes, IEC and PAN details, and complete corporate addresses.
In short

Hapag-Lloyd reports that all Indian ports are now operating under the Sea Cargo Manifest and Transshipment Regulations framework. Shippers must submit accurate Sea Arrival Manifest data 96 hours before vessel arrival and maintain strict consistency across all shipping instructions, bills of lading, and customs filings.

Hapag-Lloyd has issued a vital operational warning to container shipping customers operating through Indian gateway and transshipment ports, noting that the country's Sea Cargo Manifest and Transshipment Regulations (SCMTR) framework is now fully operational nationwide. Fleet managers, NVOCCs, and logistics coordinators must immediately adapt their data submission workflows to avoid severe port delays, customs holds, and unexpected financial liabilities under the strict new Indian customs regime. This regulatory shift impacts all import and export movements, demanding flawless digital alignment between commercial shipping instructions and official customs filings across every voyage.

What are the new SCMTR documentation rules?

Under the fully implemented SCMTR framework managed by Indian Customs, carriers and cargo owners must adhere to rigorous digital reporting timelines, including the submission of the Sea Arrival Manifest for imports 96 hours before vessel arrival. Hapag-Lloyd has urged all trade partners to utilize the current transition period to audit their internal administrative processes, ensuring that Shipping Instructions, Shipping Bills, House Bills of Lading, and official SCMTR filings match with absolute precision. Essential data fields now under intense customs scrutiny include valid Harmonized System of Nomenclature codes, Importer-Exporter Code details, Permanent Account Numbers, and complete corporate addresses. Any discrepancies in these mandatory fields risk halting cargo clearance or forcing manual intervention from terminal operators.

The regulatory burden falls heavily on non-vessel operating common carriers, consolidators, and freight forwarders, who must complete independent SCMTR registrations and establish proper bonding requirements with Indian authorities. Shippers who submit inaccurate data or miss critical filing windows face significant operational penalties. According to Container News, Hapag-Lloyd confirmed that parties responsible for late or erroneous submissions will be forced to file the Export General Manifest directly with Indian Customs at their own expense, removing the carrier as an administrative buffer.

  • Ensure Sea Arrival Manifest data is transmitted at least 96 hours prior to vessel arrival at any Indian port.
  • Cross-check all HSN codes, IEC numbers, and PAN details against official documentation before submission.
  • Verify that NVOCC bond requirements and direct SCMTR portal registrations are fully active.
  • Maintain strict consistency across House Bills of Lading, Shipping Bills, and carrier shipping instructions.
"Customers must submit complete and accurate Shipping Instructions on time, while ensuring that all mandatory data points remain perfectly consistent across every tier of the shipping documentation."

How does SCMTR impact export and import workflows?

The transition to the comprehensive SCMTR regime fundamentally alters how gateway and transshipment cargo moves through Indian maritime hubs by replacing legacy manifest systems with an integrated, highly auditable digital tracking structure. Exporters must ensure their Shipping Bill and Primary Cargo Identification Number details are correctly integrated into the Sea Departure Manifest before vessels depart the berth. Because Indian Customs has automated cross-checks between import manifests, transit declarations, and export logs, minor typographical errors in cargo descriptions or consignee addresses can trigger immediate detention alerts. Port operators and terminal handlers are reporting zero tolerance for late corrections, meaning logistics teams must pull forward their internal documentation cut-off times to guarantee compliance well before cargo reaches the stack.

What to watch next

Maritime operators managing Indian trade lanes should monitor three critical operational indicators over the coming months to protect schedule integrity and avoid demurrage charges:

  • Customs enforcement strictness regarding minor data mismatches in Sea Arrival Manifests and Sea Departure Manifests across major gateway ports like Nhava Sheva and Mundra.
  • Additional advisory updates from Hapag-Lloyd and other major container lines regarding NVOCC bonding verification audits.
  • Port community system processing speeds and potential bottlenecks as all terminal operators transition fully away from legacy manual manifest amendments.

Frequently asked

What is SCMTR in Indian shipping?

SCMTR stands for Sea Cargo Manifest and Transshipment Regulations, an Indian customs framework governing all import and export cargo movements through Indian gateway and transshipment ports.

What is the import manifest deadline under SCMTR?

Carriers and shippers must submit required import information for the Sea Arrival Manifest at least 96 hours before the vessel arrives at an Indian port.

Who is responsible for filing the Export General Manifest if errors occur?

If required information is submitted late or incorrectly, customers are held directly responsible for filing the Export General Manifest with Indian Customs.
