Turkish Navy ROV Locates Wreck of Lost Freighter Tugberk Imamoglu

Underwater surveys confirm the position of the coastal freighter following a fatal collision in the Sea.

Published 6 Sept 2026 3 min read By Aditya Istanbul

Turkey's navy has successfully located the wreck of the coastal freighter Tugberk Imamoglu using an ROV following a collision in the Sea.

Key takeaways
  • The Turkish navy deployed an ROV to confirm the wreck site of the lost coastal freighter Tugberk Imamoglu.
  • The freighter sank last week following a high-impact collision with a product tanker in the Sea.
  • Underwater ROV surveys allow investigators to gather vital hull and sonar data without endangering divers.
  • Maritime authorities are reviewing navigation protocols and traffic management following the fatal casualty.

A fatal collision in the Sea has claimed a coastal freighter, prompting an immediate search and rescue response from regional authorities. Fleet managers and maritime safety officers are closely monitoring the unfolding investigation into the incident, which underscores ongoing collision avoidance challenges in high-density coastal waters. Commercial shipping routes in the region face heightened scrutiny as maritime administrations review vessel traffic management protocols following the loss of the Turkish vessel.

How ROV Technology Confirmed Wreck Site

The Turkish Navy utilized advanced remotely operated vehicle (ROV) technology to positively identify and survey the submerged hull of the Tugberk Imamoglu following its collision with a product tanker last week. According to Maritime Executive Regs, naval diving teams and underwater drone operators mapped the exact coordinates of the wreck site in the Sea, providing essential structural data for salvage engineers and maritime investigators. Deploying deepwater ROV systems allows authorities to bypass hazardous diver-led site assessments in heavy currents, capturing high-resolution multibeam sonar imaging and video evidence of the impact zone. This rapid underwater verification ensures that hydrographic offices can update navigational warnings for transiting commercial traffic while investigators begin piecing down the sequence of navigation errors leading up to the fatal impact.

Underwater inspections of this nature are critical for determining structural breach patterns and environmental safety risks. Survey teams must evaluate whether any bunker fuel or cargo has leaked into the surrounding marine ecosystem, triggering immediate MARPOL compliance protocols. Flag state inspectors and class societies rely on these high-definition ROV surveys to substantiate navigational log data against actual physical damage observed on the sunken coastal freighter.

Commercial and Safety Implications for Fleet Managers

Maritime operators running coastal freighters and product tankers in congested waters must immediately review bridge team management and watchkeeping procedures to mitigate collision risks. The sudden loss of the Tugberk Imamoglu highlights the persistent vulnerability of smaller tonnage vessels operating alongside larger commercial tankers in busy traffic separation schemes. Charterers and shipowners should verify that their masters and mates strictly adhere to collision regulations, particularly regarding radar plotting, AIS tracking, and VHF communication protocols in restricted visibility or high-traffic corridors.

Port authorities and coastal state regulators are likely to enforce stricter navigational surveillance and speed restrictions in the affected region. Fleet superintendents should take the following operational steps to safeguard their vessels:

  • Conduct immediate navigational audits focused on officer of the watch (OOW) bridge resource management.
  • Verify that all radar, ARPA, and AIS equipment undergo preventative maintenance and class verification.
  • Review voyage planning risk assessments for high-density coastal transit lanes and pilotage waters.
  • Ensure P&I club notification procedures are fully understood by junior and senior deck officers alike.

What to watch next

Three critical milestones will shape the fallout from this maritime casualty over the coming weeks. First, monitor the official casualty investigation report released by the Turkish maritime administration regarding the precise radar and VTS logs of both vessels. Second, watch for any new port state control (PSC) inspection blitzes targeting bridge team compliance and navigational equipment across regional shipping hubs. Third, track potential amendments to local traffic separation scheme management guidelines designed to prevent future collisions between coastal freighters and product tankers.
