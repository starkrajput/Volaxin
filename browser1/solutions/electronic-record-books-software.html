Electronic Record Book Software (e-ORB)

Why Paper Record Books Keep Failing Inspections

The paper Oil Record Book asks a tired engineer, at the end of a long watch, to recall the correct code letter, the exact quantity, the tank identities and the retained balance — and to write it legibly, in ink, at the time of the operation. The predictable results are the findings inspectors record over and over:

  • The wrong code letter — sludge (Code C) logged as bilge water (Code D).
  • Quantities or tank identities omitted, so nothing can be verified.
  • Entries written up retrospectively — visibly one hand, one pen, one sitting.
  • Retained quantities that no longer reconcile with the sounding book.
  • Missing officer or Master signatures on completed pages.

None of these are knowledge failures. They are interface failures — and that is exactly what software fixes. For the full breakdown of the errors themselves, see our Oil Record Book entries guide.

How Volaxin's Electronic Record Books Work

Guided Operations, Not Blank Pages

The operator selects what they did — landed sludge ashore, transferred between tanks, bunkered — and the system applies the correct code. The C-versus-D decision stops being a memory test.

Mandatory Fields

An entry cannot be completed without the quantity, the tanks involved and the resulting retained quantity. The most common category of deficiency becomes structurally impossible.

Automatic Running Balances

Tank balances carry forward and are calculated, not re-derived by hand — so figures cannot quietly drift out of reconciliation between entries.

Tamper-Evident Audit Trail

Every entry carries the user, the time it was actually made, and a full history of any correction. Retrospective completion is not something the system can produce.

Proper Corrections

Amendments are recorded as amendments — original value, new value, who and why — matching the intent of a struck-through, initialled paper correction rather than hiding it.

Role-Based Signatures

Officer-in-charge and Master approvals are captured per entry and per period, with no ambiguity about who signed what.

Full Offline Operation

Everything above works with zero connectivity and reconciles with the office automatically when a link returns — statutory compliance never depends on bandwidth.

Fleet-Wide Office Visibility

Superintendents and the DPA can review records across the fleet, spot gaps before an inspector does, and run the same reconciliation a PSC officer would.

What Changes in Practice

  • Whole categories of deficiency disappear — wrong codes, missing quantities, unsigned pages and non-contemporaneous entries are prevented, not corrected.
  • Less time, less friction — guided entry is faster than a paper book, so records get made at the operation instead of at the desk.
  • Self-audit before inspection — run the ORB-versus-soundings-versus-bunkers reconciliation yourself, monthly.
  • Legible, complete, retrievable — no handwriting disputes, and instant retrieval for the retention period.
  • One dataset with the rest of the fleet — records connect to maintenance history and SHEQ evidence rather than sitting in a silo.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is electronic record book software?

A digital replacement for statutory paper log books that guides each entry, enforces mandatory fields, maintains running balances and keeps a tamper-evident audit trail — permitted in lieu of hard copy under IMO Resolution MEPC.312(74).

Which record books can be kept electronically?

Oil Record Book Parts I and II, Cargo Record Book, Garbage Record Book Parts I and II, Ozone-Depleting Substances Record Book and NOx Technical Code records. Confirm exact scope against current regulation and flag guidance.

Do we need flag State approval?

Yes — a Declaration of MARPOL Electronic Record Book issued by your flag Administration, carried on board. Conditions vary by registry.

Does it work offline?

Yes — full offline operation on board, with automatic synchronisation to the office when connectivity returns.
