Venergy Maritime Expands Hengli Suezmax Orderbook to 30 Ships

Greek shipowner Venergy Maritime exercises options for two more 158,000 dwt tankers at China's Hengli Shipbuilding, cementing a massive fleet expansion.

Greek shipowner Venergy Maritime has firmed up two more suezmax tanker newbuildings at China's Hengli Shipbuilding, bringing its active series at the Dalian yard to four vessels and expanding its total newbuilding programme to 30 ships. Led by Vyron Vasileiadis, the Piraeus-based owner exercised existing options tied to an earlier contract signed this year. According to Splash247, the latest additions comprise 158,000 dwt crude carriers, reflecting a continued appetite among independent Greek owners for modern, large-scale tonnage despite high asset prices across major Asian shipyards. This fresh commitment underscores how privately backed operators are securing forward delivery slots to position themselves for evolving crude trade routes and tightening environmental regulations.

How Venergy Maritime is scaling its fleet at Hengli Shipbuilding

Venergy Maritime is methodically executing a high-stakes fleet renewal and expansion strategy centered on Chinese shipbuilding capacity, specifically utilizing Hengli Shipbuilding in Dalian to scale up its operational footprint. By converting prior options into firm contracts for two 158,000 dwt suezmax tankers, the Vyron Vasileiadis-led enterprise now maintains a quartet of identical units under construction at the facility. This targeted investment forms the backbone of a broader newbuilding programme that has now reached 30 vessels, positioning the company as one of the more aggressive mid-sized growth players in the private Greek shipping sector. Shipbroking sources indicate that these modern crude carriers command significant capital outlay, yet owners continue to lock in berths as yard slots for prompt-to-mid-decade deliveries remain scarce across Tier-1 Chinese yards.

What is driving the strong demand for suezmax newbuildings?

Suezmax tankers remain a preferred asset class for commercial operators navigating shifting global crude oil trades, balancing cargo flexibility with optimal draft restrictions for major transit chokepoints. Shipowners like Venergy Maritime are committing substantial capital to 158,000 dwt designs to capture robust spot and period charter rates while phasing out older, less efficient units ahead of stricter carbon intensity regulations. Yard capacity constraints at key Chinese facilities like Hengli Shipbuilding in Dalian have compressed available construction slots well into the late 2020s, forcing charter-conscious owners to act decisively on options or risk losing preferred delivery windows. The commercial rationale relies on securing eco-friendly vessel specifications that comply fully with upcoming IMO efficiency mandates, ensuring long-term asset liquidity in competitive global crude transportation markets.

What to watch next

Industry stakeholders tracking Venergy Maritime and the wider suezmax sector should monitor three upcoming operational and commercial developments over the coming quarters:

  • Further announcements regarding the remaining vessels inside Venergy's expansive 30-ship newbuilding programme.
  • Steel-cutting ceremonies and milestone construction updates from Hengli Shipbuilding in Dalian for the 158,000 dwt series.
  • Brokers' reports on potential resale interest or charter party fixtures for these newly firmed crude carriers ahead of delivery.
