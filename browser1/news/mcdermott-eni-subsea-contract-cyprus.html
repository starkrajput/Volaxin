McDermott Lands First Eni Subsea Contract for Cyprus Offshore Project

U.S. offshore engineering heavyweight breaks into the Cypriot market via a landmark subsea award from energy major Eni.

Published 8 Sept 2026 2 min read By Marcus Nicosia

U.S. offshore contractor McDermott has secured its debut subsea contract with energy giant Eni, expanding its regional footprint to Cyprus.

Key takeaways
  • U.S. contractor McDermott secured its debut subsea contract with energy major Eni.
  • The project expands McDermott's operational footprint directly into Cyprus.
  • The deal reinforces deepwater engineering activity within the Eastern Mediterranean energy basin.
  • Local maritime supply chains and support vessel operators in Cyprus stand to benefit from the award.
In short

U.S. offshore engineering contractor McDermott has secured its first subsea contract with energy major Eni, marking its operational entry into Cyprus and expanding its footprint in the Eastern Mediterranean offshore sector.

What this article covers
  1. What Does This Eni Subsea Award Mean for Cyprus?
  2. How Will McDermott Execute the Cyprus Subsea Scope?

When an energy major awards a debut contract in a new operating basin, the commercial ripples are felt throughout the offshore supply chain. In this case, U.S.-headquartered engineering and construction specialist McDermott has officially broken ground in the Eastern Mediterranean by securing its first-ever subsea infrastructure contract with Italian energy giant Eni for operations offshore Cyprus. For fleet managers, offshore vessel operators, and marine engineering firms, this award signals a fresh wave of subsea construction activity in an increasingly strategic gas province. According to Offshore Energy, the milestone deal bridges McDermott's global offshore execution capabilities with Eni's expanding exploration and development portfolio in the region, bringing specialized construction vessels and technical spreads into Cypriot waters.

What Does This Eni Subsea Award Mean for Cyprus?

The entry of McDermott into the Cypriot offshore arena through this Eni subsea contract marks a pivotal maturity phase for the nation's hydrocarbon and energy infrastructure timeline. Cyprus has long held promise as a gas-rich province in the Eastern Mediterranean, yet translating exploration success into complex subsea production systems requires tier-one engineering capability. By deploying its specialized project management and offshore construction resources to support Eni, McDermott validates the commercial viability and logistical readiness of the local operating environment. Commercial operators, port authorities in Limassol, and regional support vessel providers must now scale up their capabilities to handle the sophisticated pipelay, umbilicals, and subsea structure installations that accompany tier-one deepwater developments.

  • Strategic expansion of McDermott into the Eastern Mediterranean offshore basin.
  • Deepening operational ties between major contractor McDermott and energy titan Eni.
  • Catalyst for local maritime supply chain development and offshore vessel chartering in Cyprus.
"Entering a new offshore theater with an established operator like Eni demands absolute precision in asset utilization, vessel coordination, and regulatory compliance."

How Will McDermott Execute the Cyprus Subsea Scope?

Executing a complex subsea scope for Eni offshore Cyprus will require McDermott to deploy advanced offshore construction vessels, remotely operated vehicles, and engineering personnel capable of navigating deepwater technical challenges. While specific vessel spreads and contract financial values remain confidential, projects of this magnitude typically involve heavy-lift construction vessels, dynamic positioning pipelayers, and comprehensive subsea umbilicals, risers, and flowlines installation spreads. Fleet superintendents and marine spread managers tracking this development note that successful execution in the Cypriot exclusive economic zone sets a operational baseline for future gas field tie-backs, subsea manifold installations, and potential carbon capture or green hydrogen infrastructure integration across the wider Mediterranean basin.
