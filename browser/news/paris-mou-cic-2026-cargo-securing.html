Paris MoU Launches 2026 Cargo Securing Inspection Campaign

Inspectors will focus on lashing equipment condition and the Cargo Securing Manual from September through November.

Published 4 Sept 2026 6 min read By Marcus Rotterdam

A concentrated inspection campaign on cargo securing runs across Paris and Tokyo MoU regions. Here is the checklist that decides whether your ship is detained.

Key takeaways
  • The campaign runs September to November 2026 across Paris and Tokyo MoU regions.
  • The most common failure is equipment aboard not matching the approved Cargo Securing Manual.
  • Officers test whether duty crew can locate the manual and explain the current securing arrangement.
  • Equipment substitutions need a documented manual amendment, not an informal swap.
In short

The Paris MoU concentrated inspection campaign on cargo securing runs September to November 2026. Inspectors verify the approved Cargo Securing Manual against equipment actually aboard, check lashing condition against rejection criteria, and test whether duty officers can explain the arrangement.

What this article covers
  1. What inspectors will actually check
  2. How to prepare before arrival

The campaign is confirmed.

What inspectors will actually check

The concentrated inspection campaign targets the approved Cargo Securing Manual and the physical condition of the lashing equipment it specifies. Port State Control officers will compare the manual against what is actually aboard, which is where most ships fail: the manual lists equipment that has since been replaced with a different type or has corroded beyond its rejection criteria. Officers will also check that crew can locate the manual and explain the securing arrangement for the current cargo. A manual that is approved but unavailable on the bridge or unknown to the duty officer produces a deficiency even when the physical lashings are sound.

How to prepare before arrival

Run a self-check against the manual at least one voyage ahead of a Paris MoU call. Physically inventory lashing bars, turnbuckles and twistlocks against the specification, and photograph anything with visible corrosion or deformation for the maintenance record. Confirm the manual reflects any equipment substitution through a documented amendment rather than an informal swap. Brief the duty officers so they can walk an inspector through the securing arrangement without hesitation, and make sure the manual is physically accessible on the bridge rather than filed away.
