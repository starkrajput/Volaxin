X-Press Feeders and T.S. Lines Expand Regional Container Services

New intra-regional loops target Indian Subcontinent transshipment hubs and Red Sea trade lanes to bypass main-line bottlenecks.

Published 6 Sept 2026 3 min read By Aditya

X-Press Feeders and T.S. Lines have launched new regional container services connecting Sri Lanka, India, Saudi Arabia, and Egypt.

Key takeaways
  • X-Press Feeders launched the Malabar X-Press (MBX) service using a 1,100 TEU containership.
  • The MBX rotation connects Colombo, New Mangalore, Cochin, and returns to Colombo.
  • T.S. Lines operates its RSS service deploying a single 4,400 TEU containership.
  • The RSS shuttle provides a direct intra-Red Sea connection between Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, and Sokhna, Egypt.
In short

X-Press Feeders and T.S. Lines have launched new regional container services covering the Indian Subcontinent and Red Sea markets. X-Press Feeders introduced the MBX shuttle connecting Colombo, New Mangalore, and Cochin with a 1,100 TEU ship, while T.S. Lines launched the RSS shuttle linking Jeddah and Sokhna with a 4,400 TEU vessel.

When main-line operators look for reliable feedering and resilient regional loops amid ongoing Red Sea routing disruptions, the Indian Subcontinent and Middle Eastern corridors offer immediate testing grounds. Fleet managers and charterers are watching closely as carriers adapt their network architectures to capture rising intra-Asian and regional volumes. This operational shift directly impacts feeder schedule reliability, port terminal congestion, and charter party execution for regional tonnage.

According to Container News, X-Press Feeders and T.S. Lines have introduced dedicated regional container services targeting the Indian Subcontinent and Red Sea markets respectively. These localized shuttles provide vital feeder connections and alternative routing options for cargo owners navigating tighter vessel availability. The deployment of specific vessel sizes on these loops highlights the tactical approach carriers are taking to match regional parcel sizes with terminal infrastructure constraints.

How Does the Malabar X Press Service Operate?

X-Press Feeders operates the Malabar X-Press (MBX) service as a weekly shuttle connecting Colombo in Sri Lanka with the southwest Indian ports of New Mangalore and Cochin. The dedicated loop deploys a single 1,100 TEU containership to maintain a strict rotation of Colombo, New Mangalore, Cochin, and returning to Colombo. This localized setup gives regional exporters and importers a reliable feedering pipeline into Colombo's major transshipment hub, mitigating landside bottlenecks and tightening transit times across the Palk Strait.

For terminal operators in Cochin and New Mangalore, the weekly MBX call brings consistent box throughput without overwhelming berth capacity. Charterers utilizing this loop benefit from fixed-day departures, which help keep laytime calculations predictable and reduce the risk of costly demurrage disputes at busy Indian gateways. As regional manufacturing hubs push for more direct linkages to primary trunk routes, dedicated single-ship shuttles offer the flexibility that large panamax vessels simply cannot match in shallow-draft regional berths.

What Route Does the T.S. Lines Red Sea Shuttle Serve?

T.S. Lines runs its RSS service as an intra-Red Sea shuttle connecting the Saudi Arabian port of Jeddah with Sokhna in Egypt. This deployment utilizes a larger 4,400 TEU containership, reflecting the heavier cargo volumes moving across this strategic maritime chokepoint despite ongoing security and routing challenges in the wider region. By focusing exclusively on the Jeddah-Sokhna leg, T.S. Lines provides a vital cross-gulf connection for boxes transiting the northern Red Sea corridor.

Operating a 4,400 TEU unit in an intra-regional shuttle demonstrates a clear appetite to capture spillover volumes from mainline vessels that have altered their schedules to avoid security risks further south. Port operators in Sokhna and Jeddah must manage these concentrated capacity injections carefully to prevent yard congestion and maintain crane productivity across busy container terminals.

What to Watch Next

Fleet managers, charterers, and port operators should monitor three key operational signals over the coming quarters as these regional networks bed down:

First, watch for schedule reliability metrics on the Colombo-centric MBX loop, particularly regarding berth availability at New Mangalore and Cochin during peak monsoon seasons. Second, track any capacity adjustments or vessel upscaling on the T.S. Lines RSS service should security conditions or trade flows shift within the Red Sea corridor. Third, observe whether competing non-vessel operating common carriers and regional lines introduce parallel feeder loops to challenge these new rotations.
