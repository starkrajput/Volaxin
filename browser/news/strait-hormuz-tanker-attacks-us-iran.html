Strait of Hormuz Tanker Attacks Spike in Unprecedented US-Iran Clash

Ten vessels struck near critical chokepoint following the sinking of five Iranian oil tankers, triggering severe safety concerns for fleet managers.

Published 9 Sept 2026 2 min read By Aditya

A dramatic escalation near the Strait of Hormuz saw ten tankers attacked by Iran after the U.S. sank five Iranian vessels, rattling maritime security.

Key takeaways
  • Iran launched attacks against ten commercial ships near the Strait of Hormuz.
  • The strikes followed the U.S. military sinking five Iranian oil tankers.
  • This represents the largest wave of tit-for-tat shipping attacks in the six-month-old war.
  • Fleet managers are facing extreme safety pressures and escalating war risk considerations in the Persian Gulf.
In short

Iran attacked ten commercial ships near the Strait of Hormuz following the sinking of five Iranian oil tankers by the United States. This marks the largest wave of tit-for-tat maritime attacks since the six-month-old conflict began, significantly increasing war risks for commercial fleet operations in the Persian Gulf.

When geopolitical flashpoints boil over into active kinetic exchanges, the commercial shipping lanes carrying a massive share of the world's energy supplies become collateral damage. Fleet managers and charterers operating crude and product carriers in the Middle East are staring down an unprecedented operational crisis following a massive spike in hostilities near the Strait of Hormuz. According to gCaptain, the Iranian government stated it struck ten commercial ships in the area. This dramatic escalation occurred directly in retaliation for United States military actions that resulted in the sinking of five Iranian oil tankers. For vessel operators navigating these volatile waters, this six-month-old conflict has officially shifted from a background risk into an active, high-casualty theater of operations requiring immediate route reassessments and heightened security protocols.

The sheer scale of this latest maritime assault marks the single largest coordinated wave of attacks against merchant shipping since the current conflict began. Risk management teams aboard tankers transiting the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman are revising their threat assessments as naval engagements increasingly spill over into commercial sea lanes. P&I clubs and hull underwriters are closely monitoring these developments, as war risk premiums for voyages entering the Persian Gulf threaten to spike dramatically. Shipowners must now weigh the commercial pressures of charter party obligations against the absolute safety of their crews, with many evaluating whether to suspend transits through the Strait of Hormuz entirely until naval escorts or reliable security guarantees are established by coalition forces.
