ABS Certifies Hanwha Ocean FPSO Design for Deepwater Plays

New floating production vessel concept clears regulatory hurdles for harsh operating environments in South America and West Africa.

Published 9 Sept 2026 3 min read By Marcus

Hanwha Ocean secures basic design review certification from ABS for an advanced FPSO unit tailored to complex deepwater offshore basins.

Key takeaways
  • Hanwha Ocean secured basic design review certification from class society ABS for a new FPSO design.
  • The certified floating production unit targets deepwater oil and gas plays in South America and West Africa.
  • The regulatory milestone allows offshore operators to fast-track front-end engineering design phases.
  • ABS certification helps de-risk early-stage engineering and streamlines future shipyard construction contracts.
In short

South Korea’s Hanwha Ocean has secured basic design review certification from ABS for a new deepwater FPSO design targeted at major offshore basins in South America and West Africa, streamlining regulatory compliance and engineering timelines for field operators.

When deepwater field operators demand faster time-to-market without compromising class compliance, pre-approved vessel designs change the commercial calculus. South Korea’s Hanwha Ocean has just secured a crucial basic design review (BDR) certification from the American Bureau of Shipping (ABS) for a newly engineered Floating Production, Storage, and Offloading (FPSO) unit, according to Offshore Energy. This regulatory milestone clears the naval architecture and foundational engineering for deployment in major hydrocarbon provinces across South America and West Africa.

For fleet managers and offshore asset owners navigating stringent class requirements, this certification de-risks the early engineering phase of capital-intensive offshore projects. The approval demonstrates that Hanwha Ocean's structural and safety blueprints meet modern maritime standards for severe metocean conditions. Consequently, project developers can fast-track front-end engineering design (FEED) phases, bypassing months of preliminary regulatory back-and-forth before heading to the shipyards for construction contracts.

What makes this FPSO design unique for deepwater operations?

Hanwha Ocean engineered its certified FPSO design specifically to handle the high-capacity topsides and harsh mooring loads typical of ultra-deepwater plays off the coasts of Brazil, Guyana, and West African nations. These regions present severe metocean challenges, including loop currents, unpredictable squalls, and massive production throughput requirements that push traditional hull designs to their limits. By securing ABS basic design review certification, the shipbuilder proves its naval architecture can safely manage heavy crude separation, massive water-injection modules, and high-pressure riser configurations without structural compromise. Asset managers evaluating these units benefit from reduced integration risks and standardized safety margins that satisfy multiple coastal state regulators in the Atlantic basin.

  • Targeted directly at high-capacity production in South American and West African deepwater basins.
  • Developed by major South Korean shipbuilder Hanwha Ocean.
  • Streamlines future front-end engineering design (FEED) cycles for offshore operators.

How does BDR certification impact commercial timelines?

Securing a basic design review certification directly compresses project development schedules by removing redundant plan-approval steps during the early phases of commercial tendering. Offshore operators and charterers can reference the ABS-approved baseline package when drafting charter parties or ordering long-lead items, saving vital weeks in competitive bidding scenarios. This regulatory clearance bridges the gap between conceptual naval architecture and final investment decision (FID) milestones, giving field developers greater certainty over shipyard delivery slots. Ultimately, pre-certified designs reduce contingency budgets allocated for class-mandated engineering changes, protecting profit margins across complex multi-billion-dollar offshore developments.

What to watch next

Tracking the commercial adoption of pre-certified offshore assets requires monitoring specific upcoming milestones across Asian shipyards and Atlantic basin projects. Industry stakeholders should evaluate three concrete signals over the coming quarters:

  • First Construction Contracts: Watch for engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) orders placed by major energy operators utilizing the Hanwha Ocean BDR framework.
  • Topsides Integration Plans: Monitor how upcoming tenders accommodate heavy production modules alongside the certified hull design without triggering major class re-evaluations.
  • Regional Regulatory Sign-Offs: Observe whether local energy authorities in Brazil and West African jurisdictions grant expedited approvals based on the existing ABS certification.

Basic design review certification is a formal assessment by a classification society like ABS verifying that a vessel's naval architecture and foundational engineering comply with applicable maritime rules and safety standards before construction begins.
