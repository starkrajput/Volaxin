APM Terminals Signs MoU for Nigeria Badagry Port Project

Maersk's terminal arm targets West African transshipment hubs through exclusive greenfield port negotiations.

Published 9 Sept 2026 3 min read By Marcus Copenhagen

APM Terminals has signed an MoU to explore the greenfield Badagry Port development in Nigeria, aiming to boost deepwater capacity and regional transshipment.

Key takeaways
  • APM Terminals signed an MoU to explore the greenfield Badagry Port development project in Nigeria.
  • The agreement was signed in Copenhagen by A.P. Moller – Maersk Group CEO Vincent Clerc.
  • Didi Ndiomu signed on behalf of Badagry Port Development Ltd and Quinn McGrath Marine and Environmental Services Ltd.
  • Discussions also included extending existing port concessions at Apapa and the Onne container facility.
In short

APM Terminals has signed an MoU to explore the greenfield Badagry Port development in Nigeria. The agreement commits parties to exclusive negotiations aimed at building deepwater port capacity, accommodating larger container vessels, and expanding West African transshipment networks.

West African maritime logistics is facing a major structural shift as APM Terminals formalizes its interest in a transformative greenfield infrastructure play. The terminal operating arm of A.P. Moller – Maersk has officially entered into exclusive negotiations to evaluate the development of Badagry Port in Nigeria. This strategic memorandum of understanding was finalized during official high-level trade discussions in Copenhagen, matching long-term port operator ambitions with federal West African maritime policy goals.

Why Badagry Port Changes West African Trade

Badagry Port is designed to deliver critical deepwater capacity to Nigeria, enabling mainline container vessels to bypass draft restrictions that currently limit major West African supply chains. The project agreement, signed by A.P. Moller – Maersk Group CEO Vincent Clerc alongside Badagry Port Development Ltd Managing Director Didi Ndiomu, establishes a formal framework for exclusive project feasibility studies. By creating a dedicated deepwater gateway, the facility aims to capture lucrative regional transshipment volumes that traditionally bypass congested West African coastlines in favor of alternative hub ports. According to Container News, the initiative directly addresses long-standing draught limitations at existing Nigerian import terminals while supporting broader cargo growth and enhanced supply chain resilience across the Gulf of Guinea.

For charterers, liner operators, and regional port managers, the introduction of a new deepwater terminal alters vessel deployment strategies. Shipping lines routinely face operational bottlenecks when deploying larger tonnage to West Africa due to restrictive channel depths and landside congestion. A purpose-built deepwater hub minimizes lightering requirements and reduces turnaround times for mainline boxships.

  • Exclusive memorandum of understanding signed in Copenhagen by A.P. Moller – Maersk Group CEO Vincent Clerc.
  • Project development spearheaded locally by Badagry Port Development Ltd and Quinn McGrath Marine and Environmental Services Ltd.
  • Targeted capability includes accommodating ultra-large container vessels and expanding West African transshipment networks.
  • Parallel negotiations cover existing concession extensions at Apapa and the West Africa Container Terminal in Onne.
What Concession Extensions Mean for Operators

The Badagry Port memorandum of understanding operates alongside broader commercial negotiations involving APM Terminals' established Nigerian terminal footprints at Apapa and Onne. During the Copenhagen diplomatic meetings attended by Nigeria's Minister of Marine and Blue Economy, Adegboyega Oyetola, stakeholders evaluated contract renewals and facility upgrades for existing container assets. Balancing greenfield investment with brownfield concession extensions allows terminal operators to secure baseline revenue streams while hedging capital expenditure against future market demand in fast-growing African consumption centers.

"APM Terminals is keen to contribute to Nigeria’s economic growth and position as a trade hub in West Africa," noted Igor van den [Assen] during strategic stakeholder discussions.
What to watch next

Industry stakeholders should monitor three key developments as the Badagry Port initiative moves past its initial scoping phase. First, watch for the formal conclusion of feasibility studies regarding marine dredging requirements and breakwater engineering designs. Second, track regulatory milestones concerning the formal renewal of existing concession agreements at Apapa and the Onne container facility. Finally, observe landside infrastructure commitments from regional transport authorities to ensure road and rail connections match future terminal throughput capacities.
